# Assessment: Physician Practice Readiness

> Evaluate a physician practice's suitability for clinical trial participation, key opinion leader engagement, or pharmaceutical research partnership.

**Goal:** Identify practices with the patient population, infrastructure, and capabilities to serve as effective research sites or strategic partners.

---

## Domain: Patient Population & Volume

**Context:** The size and composition of the practice's patient base in the therapeutic area of interest
**Value:** Sufficient patient volume enables meaningful participation, timely enrollment, and therapeutic expertise
**Risk:** Insufficient volume leads to slow enrollment, limited experience, and inability to meet study obligations

### Practice: Disease-Specific Patient Base

**Context:** The number of patients with the target condition actively managed by the practice
**Value:** Concentrated expertise and sufficient enrollment potential for research participation
**Risk:** Diluted focus, slow enrollment, limited understanding of disease nuances

#### Questions

- [ ] Does the practice treat at least the minimum threshold of patients with the target condition annually?
  - *Define threshold based on study requirements (e.g., 20+ mCRPC patients for oncology trial)*

- [ ] Is the patient population representative of the broader disease epidemiology?
  - *Demographics, disease severity, comorbidity profiles match target population*

- [ ] Does the practice have access to newly diagnosed patients (not just referrals)?
  - *Important for 1L studies; assess patient flow from diagnosis*

- [ ] Is there documentation of patient volume through EMR or registry data?
  - *Verify self-reported volume with objective data sources*

- [ ] Are patients within reasonable geographic proximity to the practice site?
  - *Assess travel burden for trial participation*

### Practice: Treatment Line Distribution

**Context:** Distribution of patients across first-line, second-line, and later therapy stages
**Value:** Ensures practice can enroll for specific study phases and has experience with progression
**Risk:** Mismatch between practice patient mix and study requirements delays enrollment

#### Questions

- [ ] Does the practice have sufficient patients in the target treatment line?
  - *1L, 2L, 3L+ distribution should match study requirements*

- [ ] Is the treatment line distribution consistent with disease prevalence expectations?
  - *Flag anomalies that may indicate selection bias or referral patterns*

- [ ] Does the practice manage patients through multiple lines of therapy?
  - *Or primarily refer at progression? Important for longitudinal studies*

- [ ] Are prior therapy histories documented for patients in later lines?
  - *Essential for studies with prior treatment requirements*

- [ ] Is there visibility into patients currently on therapy who may progress?
  - *Pipeline for studies requiring treatment-experienced patients*

### Practice: Special Population Access

**Context:** Ability to enroll patients from specific subgroups needed for research
**Value:** Access to special populations enables participation in targeted studies
**Risk:** Limited access to specific subgroups restricts eligibility for many modern precision medicine trials

#### Questions

- [ ] Does the practice have access to patients with specific biomarker profiles?
  - *BRCA+, HER2+, MSI-H, etc. based on study requirements*

- [ ] Are underrepresented populations (by age, race, ethnicity) accessible?
  - *FDA and EMA increasingly require diverse enrollment*

- [ ] Does the practice treat patients across the disease severity spectrum?
  - *Early-stage to advanced; not limited to one severity tier*

- [ ] Is there access to treatment-naive patients when required?
  - *Important for first-line studies; assess referral timing*

## Domain: Biomarker Testing Capabilities

**Context:** The practice's ability to perform and utilize molecular and genetic testing
**Value:** Biomarker testing is essential for precision medicine trials and optimal treatment selection
**Risk:** Limited testing capabilities exclude practice from precision medicine studies and may indicate suboptimal care

### Practice: Genomic Testing Access

**Context:** Availability and utilization of molecular diagnostic tests
**Value:** Genomic-informed care is standard in many therapeutic areas; essential for modern trial participation
**Risk:** Lack of testing access limits eligibility for precision medicine trials and treatment optimization

#### Questions

- [ ] Does the practice have on-site or established external lab partnerships for genomic testing?
  - *Foundation, Tempus, Guardant, academic lab relationships*

- [ ] Is next-generation sequencing (NGS) available for comprehensive profiling?
  - *Single-gene tests insufficient for many modern indications*

- [ ] Are turnaround times for test results acceptable for clinical decision-making?
  - *<14 days for solid tumors; faster for aggressive diseases*

- [ ] Is liquid biopsy available as an alternative when tissue is unavailable?
  - *ctDNA testing expands testing accessibility*

- [ ] Are hereditary/germline testing capabilities available?
  - *BRCA, Lynch syndrome testing for appropriate patients*

### Practice: Timing of Assessment

**Context:** When in the patient journey biomarker testing is performed
**Value:** Early and comprehensive testing enables optimal treatment selection and trial matching
**Risk:** Late or reactive testing misses opportunities for targeted therapy and trial participation

#### Questions

- [ ] Is biomarker testing performed at initial diagnosis?
  - *Upfront testing vs. waiting for progression reduces treatment delays*

- [ ] Is reflex testing established for guideline-recommended markers?
  - *Automatic testing without separate ordering for standard markers*

- [ ] Is testing repeated at disease progression when appropriate?
  - *Resistance mutations, clonal evolution may require retesting*

- [ ] Is there a defined process for expedited testing in urgent cases?
  - *Rapid progression, limited treatment window scenarios*

### Practice: Result Utilization

**Context:** How biomarker results are integrated into treatment decisions
**Value:** Testing only adds value when results inform care; demonstrates precision medicine adoption
**Risk:** Testing without action wastes resources and indicates care gaps

#### Questions

- [ ] Are biomarker results documented in a searchable, structured format?
  - *Not just scanned PDFs; queryable for trial matching*

- [ ] Is there a multidisciplinary process for interpreting complex results?
  - *Molecular tumor board or equivalent for variant interpretation*

- [ ] Are patients with actionable results matched to appropriate trials?
  - *Systematic trial matching process for biomarker-selected studies*

- [ ] Is there tracking of biomarker-informed treatment decisions?
  - *Audit capability for quality improvement and research*

- [ ] Are results discussed with patients as part of treatment planning?
  - *Patient engagement in precision medicine decisions*

## Domain: Treatment Protocol Adherence

**Context:** How closely the practice follows evidence-based treatment guidelines and protocols
**Value:** Guideline adherence indicates quality care and ability to follow complex trial protocols
**Risk:** Non-adherent practices may struggle with protocol compliance and represent quality concerns

### Practice: Evidence-Based Selection

**Context:** Treatment decisions aligned with current clinical evidence and guidelines
**Value:** Evidence-based care optimizes outcomes and demonstrates systematic decision-making
**Risk:** Idiosyncratic prescribing suggests quality issues and unpredictable protocol compliance

#### Questions

- [ ] Do first-line treatment patterns align with NCCN/ESMO/other relevant guidelines?
  - *Compare practice prescribing to guideline recommendations*

- [ ] Are treatment modifications documented with clinical rationale?
  - *Deviations from guidelines should have documented reasoning*

- [ ] Is there consideration of patient factors in treatment selection?
  - *Performance status, comorbidities, patient preferences documented*

- [ ] Are emerging data and guideline updates incorporated in reasonable timeframes?
  - *Practice patterns should evolve with evidence; not using outdated regimens*

- [ ] Is there documentation of why specific agents are selected within a class?
  - *Beyond "physician preference"; clinical reasoning captured*

### Practice: Guideline Compliance

**Context:** Systematic adherence to care standards and quality measures
**Value:** Consistent compliance indicates disciplined practice culture compatible with research
**Risk:** Variable compliance suggests difficulty maintaining protocol adherence in trials

#### Questions

- [ ] Are required supportive care measures consistently provided?
  - *Antiemetics, growth factors, monitoring per guidelines*

- [ ] Is imaging performed at appropriate intervals?
  - *Response assessment timing per disease-specific recommendations*

- [ ] Are dose modifications made according to established criteria?
  - *Not arbitrary reductions; documented toxicity-based adjustments*

- [ ] Is there adherence to safety monitoring requirements?
  - *Labs, vital signs, adverse event assessment at required intervals*

### Practice: Documentation Standards

**Context:** Quality and completeness of medical record documentation
**Value:** Thorough documentation supports quality care, enables research, and indicates attention to detail
**Risk:** Poor documentation creates compliance risk and suggests research data quality concerns

#### Questions

- [ ] Are treatment decisions documented with sufficient clinical detail?
  - *Rationale, alternatives considered, patient discussion*

- [ ] Is adverse event documentation complete and timely?
  - *Grade, causality assessment, action taken, outcome*

- [ ] Are response assessments documented with supporting evidence?
  - *Imaging interpretation, biomarker results, clinical assessment*

- [ ] Is there documentation of patient-reported outcomes when relevant?
  - *Symptom burden, quality of life, functional status*

## Domain: Data Capture & Reporting

**Context:** The practice's infrastructure for collecting, managing, and reporting clinical data
**Value:** Strong data capabilities enable research participation and quality improvement
**Risk:** Weak data infrastructure limits research eligibility and obscures care quality

### Practice: EHR Utilization

**Context:** Effective use of electronic health records for clinical documentation and data extraction
**Value:** Well-configured EHR enables efficient data collection and reduces research burden
**Risk:** Poor EHR utilization creates manual data entry burden and quality concerns

#### Questions

- [ ] Is a certified EHR system in use with structured data entry?
  - *Not paper charts or free-text only systems*

- [ ] Can key clinical data elements be extracted programmatically?
  - *Diagnosis codes, medications, labs, procedures queryable*

- [ ] Is the EHR configured for the specific therapeutic area?
  - *Relevant templates, order sets, flowsheets for disease management*

- [ ] Is there experience with EHR-to-EDC integration or data export?
  - *Prior experience with data extraction for research*

- [ ] Are clinical staff trained on documentation standards?
  - *Consistent data entry across providers*

### Practice: Chart Completeness

**Context:** Availability of required clinical information in patient records
**Value:** Complete charts enable accurate eligibility determination and data collection
**Risk:** Missing data creates enrollment delays and quality concerns

#### Questions

- [ ] Are disease staging and prognostic factors documented?
  - *TNM staging, risk scores, relevant biomarkers at diagnosis*

- [ ] Is prior treatment history complete with dates and outcomes?
  - *All lines of therapy, response, reason for discontinuation*

- [ ] Are relevant imaging reports available in the record?
  - *Not just "imaging done"; actual radiology reports accessible*

- [ ] Are outside records integrated for referred patients?
  - *Prior treatment from other institutions documented*

### Practice: Research Data Quality

**Context:** Ability to collect and report data meeting research standards
**Value:** High-quality data collection is essential for research participation
**Risk:** Data quality issues lead to protocol deviations, queries, and potential exclusion from studies

#### Questions

- [ ] Is there prior experience with clinical trial data collection?
  - *History of successful trial participation and data quality*

- [ ] Are staff familiar with source documentation requirements?
  - *Understanding of what constitutes adequate source*

- [ ] Is there a process for timely data entry and query resolution?
  - *Research data entered within protocol-specified windows*

- [ ] Can the site support required reporting intervals?
  - *Weekly, monthly reporting if required by study*

## Domain: Practice Infrastructure

**Context:** Organizational capabilities and resources supporting research participation
**Value:** Adequate infrastructure enables successful trial conduct and partnership
**Risk:** Infrastructure gaps lead to protocol deviations, enrollment failures, and partnership strain

### Practice: Specialty Certification

**Context:** Credentials and certifications of practice physicians
**Value:** Appropriate credentials indicate expertise and eligibility for research roles
**Risk:** Credential gaps may limit eligibility for PI roles or indicate expertise concerns

#### Questions

- [ ] Are physicians board-certified in the relevant specialty?
  - *Oncology, hematology, urology, etc. as appropriate*

- [ ] Is there subspecialty expertise in the specific disease area?
  - *Breast, GU, GI oncology focus vs. general practice*

- [ ] Do physicians maintain active research credentials (GCP training)?
  - *Current training certificates; not expired*

- [ ] Are physicians affiliated with academic or research networks?
  - *NCI, NCORP, pharma network memberships*

### Practice: Academic Affiliation

**Context:** Relationships with academic medical centers and research institutions
**Value:** Academic connections indicate research orientation and enable complex trial participation
**Risk:** Isolation from academic networks may limit access to cutting-edge trials and expertise

#### Questions

- [ ] Does the practice have formal academic affiliations?
  - *Faculty appointments, teaching relationships, research collaborations*

- [ ] Is there participation in tumor boards or case conferences?
  - *Multidisciplinary review of complex cases*

- [ ] Does the practice present at or attend major scientific meetings?
  - *ASCO, ESMO, disease-specific conferences*

- [ ] Is there a track record of research publications or presentations?
  - *Abstracts, manuscripts, invited talks in the therapeutic area*

### Practice: Research Staff & Facilities

**Context:** Dedicated resources for conducting clinical research
**Value:** Experienced research staff and adequate facilities enable successful trial conduct
**Risk:** Under-resourced sites struggle with enrollment, compliance, and data quality

#### Questions

- [ ] Is there dedicated research coordinator support?
  - *Not just clinical staff doing research on the side*

- [ ] Is research coordinator experience adequate for study complexity?
  - *Years of experience, number of trials managed, therapeutic area familiarity*

- [ ] Are investigational product storage and handling capabilities adequate?
  - *Pharmacy, cold chain, controlled substance management if needed*

- [ ] Is space available for study-specific activities?
  - *Private rooms for informed consent, sample processing, study visits*

- [ ] Is there regulatory and IRB expertise on site or accessible?
  - *Regulatory submissions, continuing review, amendment handling*
