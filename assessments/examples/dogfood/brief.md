# Brief: Software Engineering Maturity Assessment

## What we are assessing

The day-to-day engineering practices of a small-to-mid-size software team
(roughly 5 to 25 engineers) that builds and operates its own services. We
want a picture of how mature the team's habits are — not the quality of any
one product, but the practices that determine whether delivery stays fast
and safe as the team and codebase grow.

## Who it is for

Engineering managers and tech leads. The questions will be answered by the
team itself (a lead plus a couple of senior engineers) in a single sitting,
so every question must be answerable yes / no / unknown without preparation
or data gathering.

## Goal

Find the riskiest gaps in everyday engineering practice and turn them into a
short, prioritized improvement backlog. A "no" on any question should point
at something concrete the team could start fixing next sprint.

## Focus areas

1. **Delivery pipeline** — how code moves from a developer's machine to
   production: version control discipline, continuous integration, and how
   releases are cut, shipped, and rolled back.
2. **Code quality** — how the team keeps the codebase workable: code review
   practice, coding standards, and how technical debt is made visible and
   paid down.
3. **Testing** — how the team knows the software works: automated test
   coverage at the right levels, and tests gating merges and releases.
4. **Operations** — how the team runs what it ships: monitoring and
   alerting, incident response, and learning from failure.

## Shape

Keep the assessment compact: 3 to 4 domains with 2 to 3 practices each, and
3 to 6 questions per practice. Favor specific, verifiable questions ("Do all
changes reach production through one automated pipeline?") over abstract
ones ("Is your pipeline good?").
